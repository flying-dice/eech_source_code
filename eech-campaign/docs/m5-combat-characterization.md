# D2: original characterization of physical combat resolution (EEDCS-46)

**Status: characterization before extraction. The observer below is source-checked (syntax, strict feature-off preprocess equality for all twelve edited game sources, Clippy, tests, and bounded C-level checks of the observer itself under AddressSanitizer). The first independent review of it found five defects, all corrected here and awaiting the same reviewer's focused pass. No native run of it has been made, so nothing here is yet a measured fact about the original; the source reading is labelled as reading.** Nothing is moved into the host by this document or by this commit: it adds a feature-gated, read-only observer and a scenario script, and describes where a seam can honestly be cut.

The ownership rule is the migration ledger's: the host owns the physical world and the physical execution of combat (what a hit does to a target's health, how far a blast reaches and how it falls off, when a target is destroyed); the campaign owns who fought whom and why, credit, loss accounting and consequences. The composition this task is built on is the reviewed Task45 runtime (`a137fc3ec`), composed as unaccepted development: Task45 has not been delivered, and nothing in the damage path was changed by it.

Two things the reading found and the observer now measures: (1) a weapon that hits land runs the blast **twice** (the crater branch calls it, then falls through into the case that calls it again), so a land hit blasts twice with the same capability; (2) the blast skips the direct-hit target only on the hit-target path. The observer's `repeats_same_weapon` counter says whether either occurs in the scenarios.

## The observer (feature `weapon-fixture`, never shipping)

`entity/mobile/mb_cmbfx.h` hooks (empty without the feature, arguments discarded) in `damage.c`, `explosn.c`, `mb_msgs.c`, `fixed.c`, `en_msgs.c`, `en_heap.c` and the six `*_updt.c` files, and `csrc/eech_cmb_fixture.c`. Every hook only reads.

* **Collisions.** All sixteen registered collision handlers are wrapped once, when the message responses are initialised, by one wrapper; `notify_local_entity` (which the `eech-sys` crate extracts verbatim) is untouched. The wrapper reads the damage modifier (from a copy of the argument list) and the receiver's health ONLY for the thirteen kinds whose handlers read that argument and hold a damage level. A bridge is notified by its segments with NO fourth argument and holds no damage level, and a cargo and a weapon hold none either: for those three the wrapper only counts the delivery (`cmb-unobserved`) and passes the original arguments through untouched, so it neither reads an absent argument nor mislabels a health of 0 as fatal.
* **Identity.** The receiver is identified across its handler by a fixture-private generation of its slot, advanced by a hook where the entity heap frees the slot (`set_free_entity`, `set_free_local_entity`); a slot freed during the handler is reported as destroyed and read for nothing else, so a slot reused by another entity during the handler is never read as the receiver.
* **Nesting.** Frames are a table of eight; every access is guarded, the real depth is retained (`depth_max`), a frame beyond the table is counted (`depth_overflow`), never written, and makes the trace report `trusted=0`.
* **Blast.** A blast call belongs to the explosion invocation it runs inside (hooks around the kill-code switch), not to a weapon's address: a land hit's second call is `with_repeated_blast`, two separate invocations by one weapon address are not.
* **Damage application.** Every entry of `damage_client_server_entity` has exactly one exit (no weapon, invulnerable, or done) and the report says whether they balance.
* **Timed health.** The six timed rolls are observed as attempts with the draws the original's condition took and, when they fire, the health they leave, in the original's order (`timed_hash`, `order_hash`).

The shared random stream is observed by the number of draws only.

It reports four ordered hashes (`damage`, `collision`, `blast`, `repair`), counters per entity kind (collisions, direct versus blast, applied, no damage, invulnerable, fatal, destroyed, already dead, damage sum and maximum, blast candidates, in range, notified, rocket kills), capability counters (real weapon versus forecast, armour-penetration versus splash versus none, draws, player targets, multiplier moved the result), per-warhead rows, blast calls by kill code, and an ordered event log of every collision, blast and repair. Through `engine:cmb_trace ()` and `engine:cmb_events ()` of the fixture module; `lua/combat-characterization.lua` runs the campaign through the public host path and writes the checkpoints (default every 60 s) and the final report as JSON lines.

## What is checked so far (source only)

* C syntax with and without the fixture macro: the twelve edited game sources, the observer and the engine glue compile (`checks/syntax-check-46-4.log`; a subset of the engine, `init.c` and the other callers are not in it).
* Strict preprocess equality: with the macro off the twelve edited sources preprocess identically to the commit before the hooks (`checks/strict-preprocess-46-5.log`); the control with the macro on differs for all twelve.
* Bounded C-level checks of the observer itself (`reference/d2-combat-characterization/c-checks/observer_checks.c`, run by `run-observer-checks.sh`), against the REAL `eech_cmb_fixture.c` over a stand-in engine, under AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer: 24 checks (a 0-argument bridge dispatch neither reads the argument list nor the bridge and is not a delivery; sixteen handlers wrapped once and not twice; a lethal hit is observed; nesting past the table is counted, retained and untrusted, with hooks running at every depth; a freed and reused slot is not read; damage entries and exits balance and an entry without an exit unbalances; a repeated blast is told by the invocation, not an address; the timed rolls count draws and hits). `checks/observer-checks-2.log` to `-4.log`: compile 0, run 0, Docker 0. The first attempt (`checks/observer-checks-1.head.log`, the first 4 KB of a 7.6 GB log) spun in AddressSanitizer's handler before any output reached the log and was stopped; it did not reproduce in three later runs nor in a run of the original, buffered configuration, and its cause is not isolated: the check now prints unbuffered and runs under a timeout.
* Default and fixture Clippy, rustfmt and the engine, module and world tests: actual Cargo and Docker 0 (`checks/clippy-46-3-*.log`, `cargo-test-46-2.log`, `fmt-46-2.log`).

## What this does not yet show

* No native result. The natural 600-second run (`lua/combat-characterization.lua`) will say which of the thirteen collision kinds, which warheads and which blast cases the campaign itself reaches; whatever it does not reach needs a controlled witness (the Task45 controlled-launch case can launch a weapon at a host-identified target; a blast near a fixed object needs its own), and an unreached path stays declared as unreached, not covered.
* Player-targeted damage (invulnerability, difficulty modifier) and the multiplayer multiplier are reached only if a scenario has a player target; the observer counts them.
* Declared observer limits: deliveries to a bridge, a cargo and a weapon are counted, not read (no damage level, and a bridge passes no modifier); the repair and fixed-restore writers are observed as D4 coupling, not moved; the sound effects `damage_client_server_entity` creates before the physics are not observed. The trace carries `trusted=0` after any nesting overflow, unbalanced delivery, unknown slot or log overflow.
* The sound effects created inside `damage_client_server_entity` and the heap-index parity that picks them are not measured; whether creating them draws from the shared stream has not been read or measured. The observer counts the draws `weapon_damage_capability` itself takes, so it does not claim to see a draw made elsewhere in the damage path.
* No seam is chosen here. A seam proposal comes after the natural and controlled counters say what the campaign actually exercises.
